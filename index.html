The Project

More Than Just Speed

Formula 1 has always been about more than just driving fast. Engineers constantly redesign cars to improve aerodynamics, performance, efficiency, and driver safety. Over the decades, these innovations have completely changed the sport.

This project explores how F1 car designs evolved and how engineering helped create the cars we see racing today.

Our Main Question

How have engineering innovations and technological advancements changed the design, speed, and safety of Formula 1 cars over time?

1950 — Present

The Evolution of F1 Cars

Explore the major design changes that shaped Formula 1 across different generations.

1950s

The Beginning

Early F1 cars featured front-mounted engines, narrow tires, and relatively simple aerodynamic designs. Engineering focused mainly on mechanical performance.

1960s

Rear-Engine Revolution

Teams moved engines behind the driver. This improved weight distribution and handling, changing the basic layout of Formula 1 cars.

1970s

The Aerodynamics Era

Wings became important for generating downforce. Ground-effect designs also used airflow beneath the car to increase grip.

1980s

Turbocharged Power

Turbochargers allowed smaller engines to produce enormous amounts of power, making engine development a major focus.

1990s

Carbon Fiber and Electronics

Lightweight carbon-fiber structures improved strength and safety. Electronic systems also became increasingly important.

2000s

Advanced Vehicle Systems

Teams developed more advanced suspension, braking, tire, and aerodynamic systems to improve performance.

2010s

The Hybrid Era

Hybrid power units combined combustion engines with energy recovery systems to improve performance and efficiency.

2020s

Modern F1 Engineering

Modern cars use advanced aerodynamics, sophisticated power units, and computer simulations to optimize performance.

Engineering Breakthroughs

Innovations That Changed F1

These technologies helped transform Formula 1 into a highly advanced engineering competition.

01 / AERODYNAMICS

Downforce and Wings

Wings and aerodynamic surfaces push cars toward the track, increasing tire grip and allowing faster cornering.

02 / POWER

Engine Technology

Engine development moved from early naturally aspirated designs to turbocharged and modern hybrid power units.

03 / MATERIALS

Carbon Fiber

Carbon fiber provides a strong and lightweight structure, helping improve vehicle performance and driver protection.

04 / SAFETY

The Halo System

The Halo is a protective structure around the cockpit designed to help protect drivers from certain impacts.

05 / ENERGY

Energy Recovery

Hybrid systems recover energy during braking and use it to provide additional power to the car.

06 / COMPUTING

Computer Simulations

Engineers use simulations and data analysis to study airflow, test designs, and make cars more efficient.
